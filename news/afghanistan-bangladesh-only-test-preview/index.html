Test cricket's rarest fixture is back. When Afghanistan and Bangladesh walked out at the Sheikh Zayed Stadium in Abu Dhabi on Friday morning, it was only the third time the two sides had ever met in the five-day game — and the ledger could not be more perfectly balanced. Two Tests, two emphatic results, one decider in the desert.

Bangladesh won the toss and chose to bat first on a dry, scorching morning, and what follows over the next five days will settle an argument that began seven years ago in Chattogram.

Match details

  • Fixture: Afghanistan vs Bangladesh, Only Test
  • Dates: 9–13 October 2026
  • Venue: Sheikh Zayed Stadium, Abu Dhabi
  • Toss: Bangladesh won the toss and elected to bat
  • Captains: Rahmat Shah (Afghanistan), Najmul Hossain Shanto (Bangladesh)
  • Umpires: Allahuddien Paleker (South Africa), Ahsan Raza (Pakistan)

Series context: two Tests, two statements

The history of this fixture is short but violent. In September 2019, Afghanistan travelled to Chattogram and produced one of the great away performances by a young Test nation, beating Bangladesh by 224 runs. Rashid Khan was the story of that match, taking five-wicket hauls in both innings as Afghanistan's spinners dismantled the hosts on a wearing pitch.

Bangladesh's answer came four years later in Dhaka, and it was even more emphatic: a 546-run demolition in June 2023, built on twin hundreds from a then-emerging Najmul Hossain Shanto. That remains one of the largest margins of victory by runs in Test history, and it redressed the balance completely.

So here we are at 1–1, on neutral ground, with neither side having managed to land a second blow. A one-off Test is an unforgiving format — there is no second chance to correct a bad session, no dead rubber to blood youngsters. Everything is compressed into five days, and the rivalry, starved of oxygen in the longest format, finally gets room to breathe.

The key battle: Afghanistan's spin against Bangladesh's survival

Strip this Test to its essence and it is a contest between Afghanistan's spin resources and a Bangladesh batting line-up that has spent years learning how to survive them. Even without Rashid Khan — rested from Test cricket on medical advice to protect his back ahead of important white-ball assignments, the Afghanistan Cricket Board confirmed — Afghanistan have named a spin-heavy attack: Qais Ahmad, Zia-ur-Rehman and Shahidullah Kamal in the XI, with only Yamin Ahmadzai and Mohammad Saleem Safi as specialist seamers.

Bangladesh, intriguingly, have gone the same way. Nayeem Hasan was drafted into the XI from the reserves to form a three-pronged spin attack alongside Mehidy Hasan Miraz and Taijul Islam, with Hasan Mahmud and Ebadot Hossain leading the pace attack. When both captains independently arrive at spin-heavy XIs, it tells you what they expect of the surface.

And the surface is expected to misbehave eventually. Shanto said on the eve of the match that he was expecting "a slow wicket which will be good for batting" early on. At the toss, he pointed to the dry surface and the extreme heat as his reasons for batting first: "The wicket is quite dry, and it is very hot as well. I think batting in the second innings will be a little difficult." With temperatures around 40 degrees Celsius forecast, the team batting last could be in for a genuine examination.

Players to watch

Rahmat Shah (Afghanistan, captain). This is Rahmat's first assignment as Afghanistan's full-time Test captain, and the most experienced red-ball batter in the side carries the dual burden of runs and leadership. Afghanistan have won only four of their 13 Tests since gaining status in 2017; Rahmat's tenure will be judged on whether he can turn rare fixtures like this into a habit of competitiveness.

Qais Ahmad (Afghanistan). In Rashid's absence, the leg-spinner becomes the attack's most potent weapon. On a surface both camps expect to turn, Qais's ability to bowl long, attacking spells could decide the middle overs of each innings.

Shahidullah Kamal (Afghanistan). The recall is earned, not gifted: 538 runs at 76.85 in the Ahmad Shah Abdali first-class tournament. If Afghanistan's top order wobbles, the middle-order batter's domestic form suggests he belongs at this level.

Najmul Hossain Shanto (Bangladesh, captain). The twin hundreds of Dhaka 2023 remain the defining innings of this fixture's short history. Shanto arrives in Abu Dhabi having led Bangladesh to a historic Test win over Australia in Darwin — and, by his own admission, determined to treat that as past: "These are new conditions against a new opponent and so we have to plan accordingly."

Mehidy Hasan Miraz (Bangladesh). The all-rounder is the kind of cricketer who decides one-off Tests: capable of a hundred, capable of a five-for, and utterly at home in Asian conditions. With Nayeem and Taijul alongside him, Mehidy can attack without carrying the entire spin workload.

Mushfiqur Rahim (Bangladesh). The veteran's experience in subcontinental-style conditions is precisely the commodity Bangladesh lacked in Chattogram in 2019. Against a young Afghan attack, his ability to bat long could be the innings that breaks the game open.

Selection questions, answered

The XIs held few shocks but several statements. Bangladesh's big call was Nayeem Hasan's inclusion from the reserves — a vote for three spinners over an extra seamer — while Towhid Hridoy, despite a triple century for Bangladesh A, missed out. Shadman Islam was given another opportunity at the top alongside Tanzid Hasan.

Afghanistan's combination is equally spin-weighted: Ismat Alam, recalled after missing the India Test, joins Qais, Zia-ur-Rehman and Shahidullah in an attack with just two specialist quicks. It is a brave selection that backs the surface to do exactly what both captains expect.

Venue dossier

The Sheikh Zayed Stadium is familiar territory for Afghanistan, who have long staged their home cricket in the UAE. October in Abu Dhabi means fierce daytime heat — the forecast touches 40 degrees — and a dry surface that has historically offered early batting value before deteriorating. Bangladesh have never played a Test in this part of the world; their only UAE experience has come in day-night white-ball cricket. Shanto was honest about the challenge without making it an alibi: "The conditions are a little hot, that will be the case. But as professional players, we don't want to see it as an excuse at all."

Three things that will decide it

  1. The fourth innings. Everything both captains said points to the pitch at its most difficult late in the game. The side batting last will need its most disciplined cricket.
  2. Spin without Rashid. Afghanistan must prove their attack has depth beyond their superstar. Qais Ahmad's series will be watched closely.
  3. Bangladesh's openers. Tanzid Hasan and Shadman Islam against the new ball will set the tone — survive the first session, and Bangladesh's deep batting can build the platform Shanto wants.
